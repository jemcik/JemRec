WHY THE CORRELATION IS THE NUMBER THAT MATTERS

"Both channels have audio" is not the same claim as "both directions were
captured". A HAL that duplicates one direction across a fake stereo pair would
pass a loudness check on both channels and produce a recording with one voice
in it. The two cases are told apart by whether the channels are the SAME signal:

  duplicated mono      correlation ~ +1.0
  two independent      correlation ~  0.0

Measured: -0.0084. The channels are statistically independent, so they carry
different audio, which on the voice-call source means the two directions.

The 15.32% identical samples are the silence at the start, where both channels
are exactly zero, not evidence of duplication.

A FINDING FOR MILESTONE 5, NOT A PROBLEM WITH THIS ONE

The channels are about 12 dB apart: left rms 349 against right rms 1383, a
ratio near 4. Both are comfortably above the noise floor and both peak in the
same order of magnitude (9.4k against 12.6k), so nothing is clipping and
nothing is inaudible.

But a recording where one party is four times quieter than the other is
unpleasant to listen back to, and the imbalance is in the HAL, not in this
code. Worth per-channel gain normalisation when the muxer is written. It should
NOT be "fixed" by touching the capture: the raw stream is the evidence, and
normalisation is a playback decision that belongs where the file is written.
