A call recorder for unrooted Android — phone and messenger calls. One APK. No root, no Shizuku, no companion app, no computer: the phone sets itself up.

<b>How it works.</b> Android's voice-call audio source carries both sides of a call, but only system-level identities may read it, among them ADB's shell user. JemRec embeds an ADB client and pairs with the phone's own Wireless debugging over 127.0.0.1. Nothing leaves the phone.

In an ADB session of a few seconds the app starts a small shell-privileged daemon, which writes each call to a file; the app is never on the audio path and holds no microphone permission. Every connection between the two is authenticated both ways.

<b>Recordings</b> are Ogg Opus files in the phone's Recordings/JemRec folder. A recording starts when the call is answered: no ringing, and a call nobody answered is not kept. Automatic mode records every call; ask-first mode records nothing until you tap Record. Messenger calls (WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, Viber) can be recorded too, both sides, app by app, on Android 14+; all but Signal tested. It tells you when something is wrong: a silent recording, a recorder that is down, a call that went unrecorded.

<b>Playing, finding, keeping.</b> A call plays in the app as its waveform. A Calls tab shows the call log, with each recording and, for an unrecorded call, why; both lists can be searched and filtered. Deleted recordings wait 30 days in Recently deleted; auto-delete, off by default, clears old recordings and very short calls, never ones marked to keep. You can keep only some people's calls, and name files with the caller - or so Honor's Phone app plays them from its call history.

<b>Three looks</b>: Memo; Deck, a pocket recorder; Multitrack, a sound editor - each light, dark or as the phone.

<b>Answering machine</b>, off until you switch it on: it answers phone calls you don't take, plays your greeting with your microphone muted and keeps the caller's message, by the same rules as recording; you can still take the call. It pauses for a day after an emergency call. Experimental, tested on one Snapdragon phone: built-in checks and a test call tell whether it works on yours.

<b>Setup</b> takes two things by hand, once: turn on Developer options, then USB debugging and Wireless debugging, and type the pairing code into JemRec's notification. The pairing survives reboots. The recorder does not: after a restart it is rebuilt by itself on the next Wi-Fi, if the phone lets JemRec start on its own. Wi-Fi is needed only to rebuild the recorder, never to record.

<b>What stays on.</b> USB and Wireless debugging stay on while JemRec is installed, turned back on if they go off: the recorder needs them. While they are on, a computer paired with the phone can connect to it on the same Wi-Fi. To turn debugging off for good, uninstall JemRec first.

<b>Requirements.</b> Android 12 or newer, and a phone whose audio hardware hands over the voice-call source. Both sides have been recorded on an Honor Magic 8 Pro, a Samsung Galaxy S20 and a OnePlus on LineageOS. The self-test in Settings → Diagnostics answers it for your phone.

<b>Recording calls is your responsibility.</b> Consent law differs by country and state. This app records what you tell it to and warns nobody on your behalf.

<b>What it does not ask for:</b> no RECORD_AUDIO, no blanket storage access, no phone state. INTERNET is required by Android for any TCP socket, including one to 127.0.0.1, which is the only address this app connects to. READ_MEDIA_AUDIO is asked for only to list recordings made before a reinstall. READ_CALL_LOG and READ_CONTACTS are optional: they tell JemRec who a call was with, and let it notice one that went unrecorded. MANAGE_ONGOING_CALLS, allowed by the recorder while exact call tracking is on, tells JemRec when a call is answered and ends. Notification access, only if you ask, hides Android's debugging notification or names a messenger call's caller; nothing else.
