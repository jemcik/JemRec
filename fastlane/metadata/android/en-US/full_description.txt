A call recorder for unrooted Android — phone and messenger calls. No root, no Shizuku, no computer: the phone sets itself up.

<b>How it works.</b> Android's voice-call audio source carries both sides of a call, but only system identities may read it, ADB's shell user among them. JemRec embeds an ADB client and pairs with the phone's own Wireless debugging over 127.0.0.1. Nothing leaves the phone.

In a short ADB session the app starts a shell-privileged daemon that writes each call to a file; the app is never on the audio path. Connections are authenticated both ways.

<b>Recordings</b> are Ogg Opus files, about 12 MB an hour, in the phone's Recordings/JemRec folder. A recording starts at the answer; a call nobody answered is not kept. Automatic mode records every call; ask-first nothing until you tap Record, in a notification or on a button over the call. Messenger calls (WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, Viber) and Google Meet can be recorded too, both sides, app by app, Android 14+; all but Signal tested. It warns of a silent or cut-short recording, a recorder down, a call not recorded, or a setting in its way.

<b>Playing, finding, keeping.</b> A call plays as its waveform, both sides in both ears, through the earpiece at your ear, and goes on with the screen off, its caller kept off the lock screen. A Calls tab shows each call: its recording, or why not; both lists have search. Deleted recordings wait 30 days in Recently deleted; auto-delete, off by default, clears old and very short ones, never kept ones. Keep only some people's calls; name files with the caller or their number, or as Honor's Phone app plays them.

<b>Three looks</b>: Memo; Deck, a pocket recorder; Multitrack, a sound editor - each light, dark or as the phone.

<b>Answering machine</b>, off until you switch it on: it answers phone calls you don't take, plays your greeting, your microphone muted, and keeps the caller's message, by the same rules as recording; you can still take the call. It pauses for a day after an emergency call. Experimental: a test call says if it works on yours.

<b>Setup</b> takes two things by hand, once: turn on Developer options, then USB and Wireless debugging (one more switch on ColorOS, OxygenOS, realme UI), and type the pairing code into JemRec's notification. The pairing survives reboots, and Android's 7-day limit if you let it; the recorder restarts on the next Wi-Fi if the phone lets JemRec run on its own - setup asks.

<b>What stays on.</b> USB debugging, turned back on if it goes off: the recorder needs it. Wireless debugging is on only while JemRec starts the recorder; then a paired computer can connect on Wi-Fi. For banking apps, turning JemRec off can also hide Developer options or turn all debugging off. Uninstall JemRec to turn debugging off for good. Start without Wi-Fi, off by default, opens a debugging port to all networks to start JemRec with no Wi-Fi until reboot.

<b>Requirements.</b> Android 12 or newer, and a phone whose audio hardware hands over the voice-call source. Both sides recorded on Honor Magic 8 Pro, Galaxy S20, S23, M34, A17 and OnePlus on LineageOS. Settings → Diagnostics has a self-test for yours.

<b>Recording calls is your responsibility.</b> Consent law differs by country and state. JemRec warns nobody on your behalf.

<b>What it does not ask for:</b> no RECORD_AUDIO, no phone state. INTERNET is needed for any TCP socket, even to 127.0.0.1, the only address JemRec uses. READ_MEDIA_AUDIO (on Android 12, READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE) is asked only to list recordings made before a reinstall. READ_CALL_LOG and READ_CONTACTS are optional: they tell JemRec who a call was with, and let it notice an unrecorded one. MANAGE_ONGOING_CALLS, allowed by the recorder while exact call tracking is on, tells JemRec when a call is answered and ends. SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW, off until asked for, draws that button. Notification access, if you ask, hides Android's debugging notification or names a messenger caller; nothing else.
